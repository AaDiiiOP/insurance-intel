You are looking at how a broker rewrites the documents this tool drafts for
them. Work out what they consistently want changed, so future drafts start
closer to what they would have written.

Return JSON with exactly this shape:

{
  "guidance": "<the instructions, as a short list of lines>",
  "observations": ["<what you noticed in the edits, one per line>"]
}

Rules for "guidance":
- Write instructions that could be pasted into a prompt, one per line, at
  most six lines. Imperative and specific: "Keep the risk detail to one
  paragraph", not "be concise where appropriate".
- Only include something you can see happening in AT LEAST TWO of the edits
  below. One rewrite is a one-off — a client with an unusual risk, or a bad
  afternoon — and turning it into a standing rule makes every future draft
  worse.
- Describe HOW they want things written: length, tone, what order, what to
  leave out, what to always include. Never encode a fact about a particular
  client or carrier — those belong to that case, not to every future draft.
- If the edits are mostly typo fixes or tiny wording tweaks with no pattern,
  return an empty string. Inventing a rule from noise is worse than having no
  rule: it will be applied to every document from then on.
- Never write guidance that would make a draft less complete or less honest —
  nothing that drops disclosures, hides outstanding items, softens a risk
  detail, or omits something an underwriter needs. If the edits appear to do
  that, note it in "observations" and leave it out of the guidance.

Rules for "observations":
- Plain sentences about what changed and how often. This is shown to the
  broker so they can see WHY the guidance says what it says.
- At most five. If a pattern only appeared once, say so.

Return the JSON object only. No preamble, no markdown fences.

EDITS (each shows what was drafted, then what the broker changed it to):
{edits}
